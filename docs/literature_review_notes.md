# Literature Review Notes

This note summarizes how the expanded literature seed supports the repository.

## Framing and Content Analysis

Entman gives the baseline definition of framing as selection and salience. Scheufele separates media frames, audience frames, and frame-building processes, which helps keep this repository focused on textual evidence rather than reader effects. Matthes and Kohring show why frame coding needs observable indicators and reliable coding rules.

Repo implication: use the codebook before making frame claims, and record decision rules for each label.

## Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies

Baker et al. model the combination of corpus linguistics and CDA. Gabrielatos and Baker show how collocation and concordance evidence can support media-representation analysis. Baker, Gabrielatos, and McEnery demonstrate a large-scale press-corpus design, while Baker and Levon warn against cherry-picking qualitative examples.

Repo implication: use metrics, collocates, and KWIC lines to guide close reading, then report examples in relation to aggregate patterns.

## Computational Text Analysis

Boumans and Trilling review automated content-analysis tools for journalism research. Grimmer and Stewart emphasize validation and interpretation when using text as data. Nicholls and Culpepper discuss the strengths and weaknesses of computational frame identification.

Repo implication: treat scripts as descriptive aids unless there is a validated annotation set and evaluation procedure.

## Stance, Evidence, and Source Relations

Biber and Finegan support feature-based analysis of stance marking. Stance-detection surveys by Kucuk and Can and by Aldayel and Magdy clarify why stance should not be collapsed into sentiment. Saha et al. connect stance with evidence and claim veracity.

Repo implication: define targets explicitly, separate stance from sentiment, and keep source attribution visible.

## BRI and Regional Context

Niu and Relly provide BRI-specific framing context. Flint and Zhu frame the BRI through connectivity, cooperation, and hegemonic competition. Chang and Shekhar help interpret strategic and hedging language in Asian regional contexts.

Repo implication: interpret BRI media patterns with geopolitical caution rather than turning lexical frequency into simple pro/anti labels.
