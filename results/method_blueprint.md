# Method Blueprint

This file turns the literature seed table into practical design guidance.

Use it as a checklist when moving from a thesis-style question to a reproducible computational-linguistics workflow.

## Theory

### Entman, R. M. 1993. Framing: Toward Clarification of a Fractured Paradigm.

- Publication: Journal of Communication, 1993
- Method signal: frame definition
- Why it matters: Gives the theoretical basis for operationalizing media frames.
- Researcher action: Define frames before counting words; decide what selection salience problem definition and evaluation mean in your corpus.

### Scheufele, D. A. 1999. Framing as a Theory of Media Effects.

- Publication: Journal of Communication, 1999
- Method signal: conceptual separation
- Why it matters: Keeps the repo from treating text patterns as audience effects.
- Researcher action: Separate textual frame detection from claims about reader interpretation.

### Scheufele, D. A. and Tewksbury, D. 2007. Framing Agenda Setting and Priming.

- Publication: Journal of Communication, 2007
- Method signal: media-effects distinction
- Why it matters: Helps prevent overclaiming when analyzing salience without audience data.
- Researcher action: Label findings as textual framing unless the project actually measures agenda setting priming or audience effects.

## Literature Review

### Matthes, J. 2009. What's in a Frame?

- Publication: Journalism and Mass Communication Quarterly, 2009
- Method signal: review-based audit
- Why it matters: Gives a cautionary checklist for transparent frame-analysis design.
- Researcher action: Use the review as a checklist for defining frame units coding rules and validity evidence.

### Kucuk, D. and Can, F. 2020. Stance Detection: A Survey.

- Publication: ACM Computing Surveys, 2020
- Method signal: survey synthesis
- Why it matters: Provides a journal-backed map for future stance-modeling extensions.
- Researcher action: Use the survey to choose target definition dataset design and evaluation measures.

### Aldayel, A. and Magdy, W. 2021. Stance detection on social media: State of the art and trends.

- Publication: Information Processing and Management, 2021
- Method signal: review synthesis
- Why it matters: Helps avoid importing social-media assumptions uncritically into news analysis.
- Researcher action: Adapt stance categories to news discourse instead of treating positivity as stance.

## Domain Context

### Niu, C. and Relly, J. E. 2021. News from China: News framing of the Belt and Road Initiative.

- Publication: Newspaper Research Journal, 2021
- Method signal: BRI frame comparison
- Why it matters: Provides a domain-specific model for BRI frame analysis.
- Researcher action: Use BRI-specific frames alongside general media-frame theory.

### Flint, C. and Zhu, C. 2019. The geopolitics of connectivity cooperation and hegemonic competition.

- Publication: Geoforum, 2019
- Method signal: geopolitical framing
- Why it matters: Supports the development-platform versus strategic-contestation frame distinction.
- Researcher action: Treat infrastructure and strategy as co-present frames rather than mutually exclusive categories.

### Chang, D. 2022. Sowing the Seeds of Discord.

- Publication: International Studies Quarterly, 2022
- Method signal: IR mechanism
- Why it matters: Helps contextualize strategic-contestation language in media coverage.
- Researcher action: Use IR literature to interpret strategic frame labels cautiously.

### Shekhar, V. 2012. ASEAN's Response to the Rise of China.

- Publication: China Report, 2012
- Method signal: regional strategy
- Why it matters: Useful background for interpreting cautious opportunity frames in Southeast Asian media.
- Researcher action: Connect textual caution to regional hedging literature without assuming one-to-one policy reflection.

## Method Design

### Baker, P. et al. 2008. A useful methodological synergy?

- Publication: Discourse and Society, 2008
- Method signal: corpus plus CDA triangulation
- Why it matters: Directly models the thesis-style workflow used by this project.
- Researcher action: Pair frequency collocation and KWIC outputs with close-reading examples before making discourse claims.

### Gabrielatos, C. and Baker, P. 2008. Fleeing sneaking flooding.

- Publication: Journal of English Linguistics, 2008
- Method signal: collocation and semantic preference
- Why it matters: A strong model for studying media representations of contested social or political actors.
- Researcher action: Use collocate lists and concordances to inspect repeated representational patterns.

### Baker, P. Gabrielatos, C. and McEnery, T. 2013. Sketching Muslims.

- Publication: Applied Linguistics, 2013
- Method signal: diachronic corpus comparison
- Why it matters: Useful template for actor naming and representation analysis in news corpora.
- Researcher action: Compare actor terms across time periods and inspect discourse prosodies around them.

## Workflow Design

### Bednarek, M. and Carr, G. 2021. Computer-assisted digital text analysis for journalism and communications research.

- Publication: Media International Australia, 2021
- Method signal: workflow bridge
- Why it matters: Useful for turning scripts into communication-research workflows rather than isolated NLP demos.
- Researcher action: Keep generated outputs human-readable and tie each script to a research question.

## Feature Design

### Biber, D. 1988. Variation across Speech and Writing.

- Publication: Cambridge University Press, 1988
- Method signal: multidimensional feature analysis
- Why it matters: Supports feature-based comparison across text types, genres, or outlets.
- Researcher action: Use distributional feature counts as a bridge between corpus metrics and register interpretation.

### Biber, D. and Finegan, E. 1989. Styles of stance in English.

- Publication: Text, 1989
- Method signal: stance marker inventory
- Why it matters: Supports richer features for hedging certainty evaluation and authorial positioning.
- Researcher action: Add modal epistemic and evaluative markers to the feature list before modeling stance.

## Annotation Design

### Matthes, J. and Kohring, M. 2008. The Content Analysis of Media Frames.

- Publication: Journal of Communication, 2008
- Method signal: indicator-based coding
- Why it matters: Supports a codebook-driven approach to frame annotation.
- Researcher action: Break each frame into observable indicators and record coder disagreements.

### Card, D. et al. 2015. The Media Frames Corpus.

- Publication: ACL Anthology, 2015
- Method signal: annotated frame dataset
- Why it matters: Informs the codebook and possible supervised frame-classification extensions.
- Researcher action: Use it as a design reference for label hierarchy and annotation documentation.

## Source Attribution

### Pareti, S. et al. 2016. Automatically Detecting and Attributing Indirect Quotations.

- Publication: ACL Anthology, 2016
- Method signal: quotation attribution
- Why it matters: Useful for studying how judgments are outsourced to experts institutions and documents.
- Researcher action: Annotate direct and indirect reported speech before making claims about source-mediated evaluation.

## Method Validation

### Baker, P. and Levon, E. 2015. Picking the right cherries?

- Publication: Discourse and Communication, 2015
- Method signal: method comparison
- Why it matters: Warns against cherry-picking concordance examples.
- Researcher action: Report how examples were selected and connect them to aggregate corpus patterns.

### Boumans, J. W. and Trilling, D. 2016. Taking stock of the toolkit.

- Publication: Digital Journalism, 2016
- Method signal: toolkit audit
- Why it matters: Supports cautious use of scripts before larger-scale NLP modeling.
- Researcher action: Document whether each method is descriptive exploratory annotation-based or predictive.

### Grimmer, J. and Stewart, B. M. 2013. Text as Data.

- Publication: Political Analysis, 2013
- Method signal: validation principle
- Why it matters: A useful guardrail for computational claims about political or media text.
- Researcher action: Validate automated outputs against human interpretation and report uncertainty.

## Modeling

### Nicholls, T. and Culpepper, P. D. 2021. Computational Identification of Media Frames.

- Publication: Political Communication, 2021
- Method signal: computational frame detection
- Why it matters: Helps design frame modeling after codebook and validation work exist.
- Researcher action: Start with interpretable labels and gold annotations before trying predictive frame models.

### Mohammad, S. et al. 2016. SemEval-2016 Task 6: Detecting Stance in Tweets.

- Publication: ACL Anthology, 2016
- Method signal: target-specific stance
- Why it matters: Useful for separating attitudes toward China BRI ASEAN governance and risk.
- Researcher action: Define stance targets explicitly before coding or modeling.

### Saha, S. et al. 2024. The Role of Stance in Assessing the Veracity of Claims.

- Publication: Computational Linguistics, 2024
- Method signal: stance-evidence relation
- Why it matters: Connects stance to evidence evaluation without collapsing it into sentiment.
- Researcher action: If adding claim analysis record who supports denies questions or reports each claim.

## Minimum Viable Research Upgrade

A researcher can turn the current demo into a stronger study by completing these steps:

1. Replace the synthetic demo corpus with licensed or private corpus data.
2. Keep a corpus log with source, date, query, and licensing fields.
3. Generate wordlists, collocates, and KWIC lines before close reading.
4. Annotate a small gold sample with the codebook.
5. Validate any automated labels against the gold sample.
6. Report representative concordance lines together with aggregate metrics.
